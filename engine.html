Air-compressed engine

A 3D-printed four-cylinder engine that runs on compressed air instead of fuel. I modeled it in SolidWorks, printed it, and I’m now building the air system, the valve train and the control code that will let it run by itself.

The Air system tab below has a live simulation of the air path I’m building, from the tank to the exhaust.

Runs on airCompressed air pushes the pistons. There is no fuel and no combustion.
Real valve trainCam-and-spring intake and exhaust valves on top of each cylinder.
Runs by itselfAn Arduino and code I write are planned to run the engine without turning it by hand.

Modeled part by part

I built the stepper motor, the motor connector, top cover and cover board, and a four-throw crankshaft with a connecting rod and a ring-grooved piston for each cylinder. I reduced the whole design by 40% so it would fit my printer.

One mechanism

Concentric, coincident, parallel and tangent mates tie the parts into a single assembly: the crankshaft, four connecting rods and pistons, the motor, its housing, the stand and the base.

Checking the motion before printing

A SolidWorks motion study turns the crankshaft with a rotary motor from 10 to 60 RPM through the 720° four-stroke cycle. It lets me check piston travel and connecting-rod movement through intake, compression, power and exhaust.

Printed, sanded and fitted

I print on a Bambu Lab P1P. The 40% scale-down made every part fit its 256 mm build volume, and I sanded and laser-cut parts to fix fit problems between the printed pieces.

Designed, now building

Running it on air

A compressor feeds air to the top of each cylinder. With the intake valve open, the air pushes the piston down; then the exhaust valve opens and the air leaves. Cam-and-spring valves switch between the two.

  • Ball valve stops the air at once.
  • Regulator with a gauge sets the pressure.
  • Relief valve vents any excess.
  • Oil goes into the line before every run.

    Planned

    An engine that runs itself

    The goal is an engine nobody has to turn by hand. I plan to program an Arduino to run it, and I’m working out which parts the Arduino will control and how my code will time them.

    • Arduino control: code I write so the engine runs on its own.
    • Fan on the crankshaft: to help lower the pressure in my wafer inspection chamber.
    • Structural and thermal FEA on the piston-cylinder support and the crankshaft, to find stress concentrations (in progress).
